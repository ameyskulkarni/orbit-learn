# subjects/spanish_b2.md

SUBJECT: Spanish B2 Conversational Fluency
LEARNER_CONTEXT: English speaker at solid B1 Spanish, targeting B2.
  Comfortable in everyday conversation and reading; needs work on
  subjunctive mood, idiomatic expressions, and complex temporal
  and conditional structures.

SESSION_SHAPE:
  - vocabulary: 1         # A new word or expression in context, then a usage question
  - grammar: 1            # A rule-application question (fill-in, transformation)
  - translation: 1        # Translate a sentence EN <-> ES
  - listening_prep: 1     # A short dialogue with a comprehension question
  - production: 1         # Write a short paragraph on a prompt

TOPICS:
  - present_subjunctive
  - imperfect_subjunctive
  - conditional_perfect
  - por_vs_para
  - ser_vs_estar
  - reflexive_verbs
  - direct_and_indirect_pronouns
  - relative_clauses
  - imperative_negative
  - false_cognates
  - idiomatic_expressions
  - connectors_and_conjunctions
  - passive_voice
  - reported_speech
  - time_expressions
  - business_vocabulary
  - travel_vocabulary
  - emotions_and_opinions

DIFFICULTY_LEVELS:
  1: Recognition. Pick the correct form or word from options.
  2: Comprehension. Understand meaning; explain the rule in simple English.
  3: Application. Produce a correct sentence in a controlled context.
  4: Analysis. Choose between subtle alternatives; explain the nuance.
  5: Synthesis. Write a natural paragraph deploying multiple target structures fluidly.

TEACHING_STYLE: |
  When teaching a topic (remediation or first introduction):
  - Start with the pattern in English: what mental move does the learner make?
  - Give ONE minimal example in Spanish with an English gloss.
  - State the rule in one sentence, then any important exceptions.
  - End with the most common mistake a B1 learner makes and how to avoid it.
  Prefer conversational, natural examples over textbook ones.
  Keep teaching blocks to 4-6 sentences. Dense, not verbose.

GRADING_RUBRIC: |
  1 - Blank, wrong tense, or unintelligible.
  2 - Understandable but grammar errors that would confuse a native speaker.
  3 - Correct core meaning; minor errors (agreement, prepositions) a native
      would notice but forgive.
  4 - Fluent and natural; sounds like an advanced learner.
  5 - Indistinguishable from a native speaker; nuance and idiom deployed correctly.
